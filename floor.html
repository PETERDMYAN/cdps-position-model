2The put price

The position carries a contractual floor of 12%, so the strike is determined, not negotiated.

Put strike: the year-4 value that floors the position IRR at 12%
128.68

The position pays 6.00 a year for 4 years and nothing else until close, so the terminal value carries the rest of the return:

A cash premium cannot be computed from these inputs. A Black-Scholes put premium needs volatility, the risk-free rate and the exercise style, none of which were given. What the inputs do pin down is the strike at which the put turns 12% from a target into a floor. If you want the traded premium instead, give me implied volatility and the rate and I will price it.
The strike sits 28.7% above spot, and that is the point. A floor of 128.68 is what 12% costs when the dividend only delivers 6%. The stock starts below the strike and stays there in three of the four scenarios, so the put is exercised and the position returns exactly 12%. Only a rally past 128.68 beats the floor. The put is not tail protection here. It is the main source of return.

3Carrying value accretion

The book value of the whole position grows at the deal IRR and is reduced by each dividend paid. This schedule is fixed at trade date and does not move with the stock.

4How each leg is reported

CDPS dividend, carried at cost

Income is recognised yearly at the coupon on original cost. It is the same number in every scenario and every year, so it carries no information about how the position is actually doing.

dividend income = cost × 6% = 6.00 per year

Put option, marked to market

Carrying value is the accreted position value less the stock price, as specified. Each year's reported option result is the change in that carrying value.

option carrying value = PV(CDPS with option) − stock price
option P&L = carrying value this year − last year

The stock price enters this formula with a minus sign. That single detail drives everything in section 5.

5Four scenarios

Each chart plots cumulative result by year. Reported follows the two rules above. Actual is cash dividends received plus the change in economic value, where economic value is the greater of the stock price and the guaranteed value of the position at that date.

Three of the four scenarios are the same deal. Because the floor binds whenever the stock ends below the strike, B, C and D all settle at 128.68 and all return exactly 12%. Their economics are identical. Everything that differs between those three charts is reporting, not performance.

8Assumptions to confirm

  • Put strike level. Set to the level that floors the position IRR at 12%, per the deal terms. Unlock the input above to test a different strike.
  • Terminal payoff. Modelled as the greater of the stock price and the strike at year 4, settled once, with no early exercise and no knock-out.
  • Economic value between trade and close. Modelled as the greater of the stock price and the contractually guaranteed value at that date, because no volatility was supplied. A full option mark would smooth the actual line but would not change its direction or the gap at close.
  • Dividend certainty. Modelled as paid in full and in cash every year. A deferral or a payment in kind would break the accretion schedule as well as the income line.
  • No counterparty reserve. The put is assumed to pay in full. Since the floor carries the return in three of the four scenarios, this is the single largest unmodelled risk on the page.
