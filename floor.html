CDPS with Put Option: Reported versus Actual

Four-year hold. Where book reporting and economic reality diverge, and what the desk needs to know.

2The put price

The position carries a contractual floor of 12%, so the strike is determined, not negotiated.

Put strike: the year-4 value that floors the position IRR at 12%
128.68

The position pays 6.00 a year for 4 years and nothing else until close, so the terminal value carries the rest of the return:

A cash premium cannot be computed from these inputs. A Black-Scholes put premium needs volatility, the risk-free rate and the exercise style, none of which were given. What the inputs do pin down is the strike at which the put turns 12% from a target into a floor. If you want the traded premium instead, give me implied volatility and the rate and I will price it.
The strike sits 28.7% above spot, and that is the point. A floor of 128.68 is what 12% costs when the dividend only delivers 6%. The stock starts below the strike and stays there in two of the three scenarios, so the put is exercised and the position returns exactly 12%. Only a rally past 128.68 beats the floor. The put is not tail protection here. It is the main source of return.

3Carrying value accretion

The book value of the whole position grows at the deal IRR and is reduced by each dividend paid. This schedule is fixed at trade date and does not move with the stock.

4How each leg is reported

CDPS dividend, carried at cost

Income is recognised yearly at the coupon on original cost. It is the same number in every scenario and every year, so it carries no information about how the position is actually doing.

dividend income = cost × 6% = 6.00 per year

Put option, marked to market

Carrying value is the present value of the expected cash flow from the whole position, less the stock price, as specified. Each year's reported option result is the change in that carrying value.

option carrying value = PV of expected cash flow from whole position − stock price
option P&L = carrying value this year − last year

Expected cash flow means the dividends still to come plus the terminal payoff, discounted at the deal IRR. The terminal payoff is taken as the strike, so this present value is exactly the accretion schedule above and it does not move with the stock. Re-estimating the terminal payoff at the current stock price each year instead would leave every figure below unchanged except the rally case.

The stock price enters this formula with a minus sign. That single detail drives the year-by-year picture below.

Closing the position in the final year

The position is closed at the end of year 4. Cash in is the settlement, the greater of the stock price and the strike. What comes off the books is the CDPS at original cost plus whatever the option leg is carried at by then, so the closing entry is the difference between the two.

close-out result = settlement − (CDPS at cost + option carrying value)

This is the entry that finally puts the stock on the books, and it is the reason the two lines meet at close.

5Three scenarios

Each table splits the reported line into its three pieces, year by year: the dividend, the option mark, and the close-out entry in the final year. Actual is cash dividends received plus the change in economic value, where economic value is the greater of the stock price and the guaranteed value of the position at that date. Each chart plots the two cumulative lines.

Once the close is booked, the books land on the right answer and only get there in the last year. Cumulative reported equals cumulative actual to the cent in all three scenarios, because the closing entry releases the frozen option carrying value against real cash. Everything that looks like a measurement error is a timing error, and the whole correction arrives in one period.

Note also that a moderate fall and a hard fall are one scenario here, not two. The brief asked for a fall that stops short of the put strike and a fall that breaks through it, but with a floor of 128.68 the stock is below the strike from day one, so both are the same case. What remains is two economic outcomes: a rally past the strike, or the floor.

8Assumptions to confirm

  • Put strike level. Set to the level that floors the position IRR at 12%, per the deal terms. Unlock the input above to test a different strike.
  • Terminal payoff. Modelled as the greater of the stock price and the strike at year 4, settled once, with no early exercise and no knock-out.
  • Closing entry. The position is closed in the final year, with the settlement received in cash and the CDPS released at original cost alongside the option carrying value. No fees, taxes or settlement lag are modelled.
  • Economic value between trade and close. Modelled as the greater of the stock price and the contractually guaranteed value at that date, because no volatility was supplied. A full option mark would smooth the actual line but would not change its direction or the gap at close.
  • Dividend certainty. Modelled as paid in full and in cash every year. A deferral or a payment in kind would break the accretion schedule as well as the income line.
  • No counterparty reserve. The put is assumed to pay in full. Since the floor carries the return in every outcome except a rally past the strike, this is the single largest unmodelled risk on the page.
