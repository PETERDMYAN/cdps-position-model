The scenarios only stay distinct if the strike is below spot, and a strike below spot means 12% is an underwriting target, not a guarantee. Both readings are modelled below. 3Carrying value accretion

The book value of the whole position grows at the deal IRR and is reduced by each dividend paid. This schedule is fixed at trade date and does not move with the stock.

4How each leg is reported

CDPS dividend, carried at cost

Income is recognised yearly at the coupon on original cost. It is the same number in every scenario and every year, so it carries no information about how the position is actually doing.

dividend income = cost × 6% = 6.00 per year

Put option, marked to market

Carrying value is the accreted position value less the stock price, as specified. Each year's reported option result is the change in that carrying value.

option carrying value = PV(CDPS with option) − stock price
option P&L = carrying value this year − last year

The stock price enters this formula with a minus sign. That single detail drives everything in section 5.

8Assumptions to confirm

  • Put strike level. Modelled at a negotiated level below spot. The term sheet decides whether it is this, or the IRR-floor strike.
  • Terminal payoff. Modelled as the greater of the stock price and the strike at year 4, settled once, with no early exercise and no knock-out.
  • Economic value between trade and close. Modelled as intrinsic value only, because no volatility was supplied. A full option mark would smooth the actual line but would not change its direction or the size of the gap at close.
  • Dividend certainty. Modelled as paid in full and in cash every year. A deferral or a payment in kind would break the accretion schedule as well as the income line.
  • No counterparty reserve. The put is assumed to pay. If the writer is the same entity whose stock is falling, the protection is worth least exactly when it is needed.
