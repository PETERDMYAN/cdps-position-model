CDP position with put · 4-year hold · illustrative

Reported P&L tells a different story from actual P&L until the day we close

The CDPs are carried at cost and their dividends are booked as income, while the put is marked to market. Reported P&L therefore leaves out every move in the CDP price until the position is sold. Over four years the two lines can sit up to S$504k apart, and the difference is booked in one go at close.

What the charts show

  • Up: for two years reported P&L is close to zero, because put losses eat the dividends, while the position has actually made over S$230k. The S$504k CDP gain only appears at close.
  • Flat: the two lines match. Dividends carry the result; put decay is heaviest in year 4, so reported P&L falls from S$73k to S$50k in the last year.
  • Down, above the strike: the put expires worthless. Reported P&L peaks at S$83k early in year 4, then the put loss and a S$39k CDP loss at close leave only S$5k. This is the weakest outcome after the deep fall, because the protection was paid for and never used.
  • Down, below the strike: reported P&L climbs to S$315k because the put gains and dividends are booked but the CDP loss is not. Closing books a S$365k loss in one day and the result turns to −S$50k. The put caps the loss.

Assumptions

  • 10,000 CDP units bought at S$100 (S$1.0m), held 4 years and sold at the end of year 4
  • Dividends 6% a year on the market price, paid quarterly, booked as income
  • Put value = PV of CDPs with the put − CDP market price, priced with Black-Scholes as above and marked quarterly
  • Up case price path set so the whole position earns a 12% IRR (+10.7% a year); the deep fall is the mirror (−10.7% a year); the mild fall is −1% a year, ending at S$96.06, just above the strike
The put cost of 19% of notional drives the size of every number here. Replace volatility and strike with the actual trade terms before using the figures externally.
